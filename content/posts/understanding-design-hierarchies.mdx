Most visual clutter is really indecisive hierarchy. When every label shouts at the same weight and contrast, readers have to work twice as hard to orient themselves.

## Three shades of grey go a long way

Instead of reaching for dozens of colors or heavy borders, a three-step neutral scale covers almost every editorial UI need:

1. **Primary ink** (`grey1`) for titles, active links, and section headers.
2. **Secondary ink** (`grey2`) for body copy, locations, and supporting context.
3. **Muted ink** (`grey3`) for timestamps, years, and platform labels.

Notice how a two-column CV row relies almost entirely on spatial alignment and those three contrast levels—no cards, no drop shadows, no badges competing for attention.

## Designing for scanning and deep reading

At a glance, the left column anchors time (`2017 — Now`), while the right column carries the story. Once you click into an essay or case study, the grid collapses gracefully into a single uninterrupted flow.
