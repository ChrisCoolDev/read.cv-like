The best digital tools rarely feel loud. Think back to the corners of the web that aged well: clean typography, predictable rhythm, and an obvious respect for the reader's time.

When we started rethinking how a personal portfolio should work, we noticed a familiar pattern. Most templates optimize for spectacle—giant hero gradients, scroll-jacked transitions, and layouts that break the moment you add a second paragraph.

## Why writing belongs next to your CV

A resume shows *where* you have been. Writing shows *how* you think.

Putting both in the same quiet column creates a natural narrative:

- **Context over buzzwords**: an essay explaining a design trade-off tells a stronger story than five bullet points.
- **Durable URLs**: Markdown files in your own repository remain readable twenty years from now.
- **Open syndication**: anyone who enjoys your work can subscribe via `/rss.xml` without algorithmic feeds in the way.

> Good software feels like a well-typeset book: nothing gets between your eyes and the ideas.

## Keeping the interface out of the way

In practice, restraint means enforcing strict typographic discipline:

```css
.reading-column {
  max-width: 540px;
  font-size: 14px;
  line-height: 1.65;
  letter-spacing: 0.01em;
}
```

When the measure stays around 60–70 characters and hierarchy relies on subtle contrast shifts (`#111` / `#555` / `#999` in light mode, `#eee` / `#bbb` / `#888` in dark mode), pages read effortlessly on both desktop and mobile.
